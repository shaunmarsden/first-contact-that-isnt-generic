# Two Drafts: Weak and Strong

Source: [inputs.md](inputs.md).

## The Weak Version, Shown for Contrast, Not to Send

Subject: Exciting opportunity for us both

"Hi Dana, I saw that Bramford Analytics is growing and looking for talented engineers. I have strong experience in backend development and I'm confident I'd be a great addition to your team. Would you have 30 minutes this week for a call to discuss how I could contribute to your goals?"

**Why this fails:** "I saw that Bramford Analytics is growing" is not a verifiable signal, it is a generic observation that could be sent to any company that has ever posted a job listing. "Strong experience in backend development" claims relevance without connecting to anything specific about these three listings. It leads straight with a meeting request before any value has been offered or earned. The subject line names the pitch directly, which reduces the odds it gets opened at all.

## The Strong Version

Subject: quick one re your backend team

"Hi Dana, noticed Bramford's posted three backend roles for the same team this month, that's usually a sign of either a big push or some real growing pains around data pipeline work specifically. I've spent the last four years deep in exactly that kind of work. Happy to send over a short write-up of a few things I'd actually look at first if I were joining that team, no strings, might be useful whether or not this goes anywhere. Worth a look?"

**Why this works:** the opener is tied to a specific, verifiable fact, three listings for the same team in one month, not a generic "you're growing" observation. The relevant experience is stated plainly without an unearned claim. The offer is small, genuinely useful, and produced quickly, not the actual thing being sought (a role). The next step is a low-friction reply, not a calendar request. The subject line is short and does not name the actual reason for reaching out.
