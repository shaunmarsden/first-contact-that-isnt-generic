# First Contact That Isn't Generic

You do not need to install anything to try this once. The lines between the dashes at the very top are just this file's label; leave them in. On GitHub, copy this using the **Raw** button near the top of the page rather than selecting the rendered text, so the tables and links below paste in cleanly. Send the whole file as your first message in any AI chat tool, then follow it with your actual inputs.

Pick a target and draft a first message worth a reply, without leading with a generic observation or an unverified hook. A message that could be sent to anyone in a similar position unchanged is not going to get a reply from this specific person.

## Gather the Inputs

- What you are actually trying to achieve by reaching out, and the specific kind of person who could help
- Whatever record already exists, so you do not reach out to the same person twice without realising
- Any actual contact detail or route to this person, and how confident it is

## Select the Target

Score a target higher when there is:

- A specific, verifiable public signal tied to the actual reason you are reaching out, not a generic observation that could apply to almost anyone in a similar position
- A workable, evidence-based route to actually reach them, not only a best-guess contact detail

Score it down when the only available hook is a generic trend or observation with nothing specific to this person or situation behind it.

## Draft the First Message

Structure:

1. A question-led opener tied to the specific signal you found, not a generic observation
2. A low-friction offer, something small and genuinely useful you can produce quickly, rather than asking for their time straight away
3. One sentence on what that offer is actually worth to them
4. A single, low-friction next step, usually a short reply, not a meeting request, unless that is a deliberate choice for this specific case

The offer is a small, genuine one, not the actual thing you ultimately want from them. Never let the first message ask for the real thing straight away.

There are two valid shapes, not one. Where nothing exists yet, promise the small offer and ask for a reply. Where something genuinely specific already exists and can be shared immediately, sharing it and asking for a short reply to their thoughts is reasonable too. Never blend the two: do not promise something that already exists, and do not ask for a meeting when nothing has actually been shared yet.

Treat the subject line as its own step. Keep it short, a few words, and never naming the actual reason for the message, since a subject line that gives away the pitch measurably reduces opens.

## Apply the Guardrails

- Never fabricate a signal or hook. If nothing specific and verifiable was found, say so rather than writing a generic opener anyway
- Never lead with a meeting or time request unless that is a deliberate choice for this specific case
- Keep the message short; a first message that reads like a pitch gets deleted, not replied to
- Never claim a capability, credential, or outcome that has not actually been confirmed
- Never invent urgency or scarcity that is not real

## Stop When the Task Is Unsafe

Do not draft a first message when:

- No verifiable signal exists and the message would rely on a generic or invented hook
- The contact route is an unverified guess and no safer route exists
- The target does not actually fit what you are trying to achieve, regardless of how easy the guess is to make

This selects a target and drafts a message. Sending it stays subject to explicit human approval.
